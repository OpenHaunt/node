# 07 — IO Board Catalog

Planned IO boards. Only the DMX board has a completed component selection; the rest are specified to the
level of "we know what it is and what it costs us".

Every board carries an RP2354, so the "bus lines used" column from the two-board design is gone. What
matters now is which rails a board needs and how much of the budget it eats.

## 0x0001 — DMX512 / RDM

The board the project started as. One isolated DMX universe on an XLR connector.

### Bill of materials

| Function | Part | Source | ~Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| MCU | **RP2354A** | Raspberry Pi distributors | ~$1.10 |
| Isolated transceiver | **Chipanalog CA-IS2092VW** | LCSC **C7422559** | $1.44 |
| Bus protection | **Littelfuse SM712-02HTG** | LCSC **C172881** | $0.42 |
| Overcurrent, optional | Bourns **MF-MSMF010-2** | LCSC **C12430** | — |
| Surge limiting, optional | 2× Panasonic **ERJ-P06F10R0V** | DigiKey | — |
| Crystal | 12 MHz | — | ~$0.10 |
| Connector | XLR, 3- or 5-pin, **not yet decided** | — | — |

The ID EEPROM is gone. The RP2354 reports the descriptor over the link.

### What the RP2354 changes here

DMX512 is a 250 kbaud frame preceded by a break of at least 92 µs and a mark-after-break. On the old design
that came out of an ESP32 UART with the break generated by reconfiguring the pin, on a chip that was also
running lwIP, an HTTP server and an mDNS responder.

A **PIO state machine generates the whole frame**, break included, with no CPU involvement and no
interaction with anything else on the chip. RDM's turnaround timing, which is tight enough to be genuinely
awkward on a busy MCU, becomes a local problem on a core doing nothing else.

The direction pin is driven by the RP2354 directly, so `MOD_UART_DE` disappears from the interface.

### Why the CA-IS2092VW

An isolated RS-485 transceiver with an integrated isolated DC/DC, so no separate isolated supply is needed.
Chosen over the industry-standard ADM2587E on price: about $1.44 against $7.35 at 100 pieces for
functionally equivalent parts.

| | CA-IS2092VW | ADM2587E |
|---|---|---|
| Isolation | 3.75 kV rms for 60 s, VDE / UL 1577 / CQC / TÜV | 2.5 kV rms |
| Data rate | 500 kbps, slew-rate limited | 500 kbps, slew-rate limited |
| ESD on bus pins | ±20 kV **HBM** | ±15 kV **IEC 61000-4-2** |
| Separate logic supply | **Yes, VCCL, 2.375 to 5.5 V** | No |
| Package | SOIC-16-WB, hand-solderable | SOIC-20W |
| Price at 100 | **$1.44** | $7.35 |

**The VW variant is essential, not a preference.** The plain W has ratiometric input thresholds on `/RE`,
where V_IH is 0.7 × VCC. At VCC = 5 V that demands 3.5 V, which a 3.3 V MCU cannot deliver. The VW adds a
separate logic supply pin `VCCL`, so `VCCL` runs at 3.3 V to match the RP2354 while `VCC` runs at 5 V to
feed the isolated DC/DC, and `SEL` picks the isolated-side supply.

Caveat: with VCC = 3.3 V, `SEL` must float or tie to GND_B, forcing VISO to 3.3 V. Only VCC = 5 V gives a
free choice.

### Why DMX works fine at 3.3 V on the bus side

Counter-intuitive enough to be worth recording. RS-485 is differential, and the receiver decides on the
A minus B difference against a ±200 mV threshold. The standard requires the driver to deliver at least 1.5 V
differential into 54 Ω. Both the CA-IS2092VW and the ADM2587E regulate their isolated side to about 3.3 V
regardless of input supply, and both meet the 1.5 V minimum. A higher supply buys nothing on the bus.

### Protection

```
XLR pin 2/3 ──▶ [PPTC] ──▶ SM712 (to GND_B) ──▶ [10 Ω] ──▶ A/B on CA-IS2092VW
```

**SM712-02HTG is always fitted.** Asymmetric −7 V / +12 V standoff matching the RS-485 common-mode range,
±30 kV IEC 61000-4-2 contact and air, 19 A at 8/20 µs, 600 W, SOT-23. At $0.42 there is no case for leaving
an XLR connector unprotected.

**Its ground pin goes to GND_B**, the isolated cable-side ground, the same reference as XLR pin 1. Tying it
to chassis or logic ground shorts out the 3.75 kV barrier the board exists to provide.

**PPTC and series resistors are footprints with 0 Ω fitted.** They are needed only if the product has to
demonstrate surge immunity per IEC 61000-4-5, which is not normally required at a signal port unless the
assessment uses the industrial generic standard and the >30 m rule applies.

If they are fitted, use **MF-MSMF010-2**: 0.1 A hold, **60 V**, 0.7 Ω, 1812. The 60 V rating is the point,
because XLR-3 is also the audio connector and someone will eventually plug in 48 V phantom power. The
commonly recommended `MF-PSMF010X-2` holds off only 15 V and is the wrong part. Series limiting is done by
the 10 Ω pulse-withstanding resistors, not by the PPTC, because a PPTC takes about 1.5 s to trip and
contributes only its 0.7 Ω during an 8/20 µs event.

Signal cost with both fitted: 2 × 10.7 Ω in series with 54 Ω leaves about 72 % of the driver swing at the
connector, roughly 1.1 V against a 200 mV receiver threshold. Formally outside the RS-485 driver spec at the
connector. DMX512-A does not test this.

### Free capabilities

The transceiver is half-duplex with direction control, so keeping RX routed costs nothing and leaves RDM and
DMX input open as pure firmware questions. Do not optimise these away in hardware.

### Open

XLR 3-pin or 5-pin. 5-pin is the DMX512-A standard, 3-pin is what the haunt and small-venue market actually
uses. Affects the board outline and the enclosure. See Q8 in [11](11-open-questions.md).

## 0x0002 — Digital input, 8×

Buttons, reed switches, PIR sensors, door contacts, prop triggers. The most common board in an escape room.

- 8 opto-isolated inputs straight into RP2354 GPIOs, no expander
- 12 V available on the terminals for powering PIRs
- Screw terminals or a pluggable terminal block
- Opto-isolation matters here. Input wiring in a haunt runs long distances alongside mains and DMX and
  arrives with whatever the environment induced onto it

The I2C expander from the two-board design is gone, and with it the I2C read in the latency chain. **The
RP2354 timestamps the edge on its own clock** and reports it with the event, so jitter in the link and the
network stack no longer accumulates into the reported time. That was a design rule the old architecture
stated but could not really enforce.

Latency is the design driver. A scare that fires late is not a scare.

## 0x0003 — Addressable LED

- PIO-driven WS2812 timing, level shifted 3.3 V to 5 V
- Terminal for external 5 V injection and a jumper to select bus-powered or externally-powered

**This board is the reason 802.3bt was worth the money.** On the af budget, 60 mA per RGB pixel at full
white meant roughly 55 pixels before the node browned out, which is not a length of LED strip anybody wants.
On `PWR-BT` the 5 V rail has about 45 W behind it, roughly 250 pixels at full white, and PIO can drive
several strings concurrently rather than the one RMT channel the old interface exposed.

**The 24 V rail changes what this board should be.** 5 V WS2812 is limited by volt drop along the strip long
before it is limited by the node's budget, which is why long runs need injection at all. 24 V addressable
strip — WS2811 in three-LED groups, or 24 V COB — draws a fifth of the current for the same power and is
what long runs actually use. At 67 W a 24 V rail is 2.8 A where 5 V would be 13.4 A: the difference between
a header and a bus bar.

**So this board wants both**: 5 V for short dense runs, 24 V for long ones, with the strip type selected by
jumper and declared in `rails_required`. That makes it the one board in the catalogue that sets
`capability_flags` bit 7, and the reason `PWR-BT4` is a 24 V build.

The constraint moves from "one fast line and 10 W" to "how many amps do you want", which is a much better
problem. Long runs still want external injection, both for volt drop along the strip and to keep the
node's own thermal budget sane.

## 0x0004 — Mains relay, 3 phases + neutral

**Read [09 — Compliance & Safety](09-compliance-and-safety.md) before designing this board.**

Interrupt a three-phase 230/400 V supply on all three lines and neutral.

**Use one four-pole device with a single coil, not four individual relays.** The neutral pole must not open
before the line conductors and must close no later than them. With four independent relays that sequencing
depends on coil tolerances, temperature and ageing, which makes a safety-relevant property a function of
component spread. A four-pole contactor has manufacturer-defined, tested pole sequencing.

Candidates: four-pole installation contactors with 12 V DC coils, such as Finder 22.44, ABB ESB or Eaton
Z-SCH. Coil power of 1.5 to 2.5 W is 125 to 210 mA at 12 V, which fits any variant's budget, but is also
2 W of continuous heat whenever energised.

Verify before selecting:

- **Pole-to-pole insulation rated for 400 V.** Many multi-pole relays specify contact-to-contact withstand
  only within one phase.
- **Creepage and clearance** per IEC 60664-1 for 400 V in overvoltage category III. Pull the values from
  the tables, do not estimate them.
- **Utilisation category.** AC-1 for resistive loads, AC-3 for motors. With switch-mode supplies as the
  load, which is most haunt equipment, inrush is the real sizing criterion rather than steady-state current.
- **De-energised state.** Should the load drop out when the node loses PoE? Safer, but "the lights went out
  because a switch rebooted" is usually the worse outcome on site. A product decision.

Bistable contactors remove the 2 W of standing coil dissipation at the cost of a second control line and a
harder question about state after a power failure.

**This board is remote, not stacked.** In practice that means it gets its own node. See
[06 — Board Interfaces](06-board-interfaces.md).

## 0x0005 — OLED display

I2C SSD1306 or SH1106, 128×64, driven by the RP2354. Countdown timers, hint display, prop instrumentation,
and a local diagnostic readout showing IP address and board status without a laptop.

## 0x0006 — Dry contact output, 4×

Four low-voltage relay or solid-state outputs for triggering third-party equipment: audio players, smoke
machines with contact-closure inputs, existing show controllers. The glue board that lets OpenHaunt drive
things it does not otherwise speak to.

A good candidate for the failsafe capability flag. A dry contact that holds its last state through a link
dropout is usually right; one that holds it through a five-minute network outage usually is not.

## 0x0007 — Environmental sensor

Temperature, humidity and air quality over I2C. Less about effects, more about operations. Fog fluid
behaviour depends on humidity, and an escape room's HVAC failing mid-season is worth knowing about before
the guests mention it.
